About the temple

An ancient shrine by
the Neyyar River

Krishnapuram Brahmana Samudayam, Sri Krishna Swamy Temple, Neyyattinkara, is an ancient and powerful shrine in south Kerala dedicated to Lord Sri Krishna. The temple is situated in beautiful, serene and natural green surroundings by the side of the Neyyar River.

"The idol which was planned for a new temple was installed here, since this powerful deity had to face east — on the banks of the Neyyar river, surrounded with vedic mantras."

The history behind the construction of this temple is that the Travancore prince Anizhamthirunal Marthanda Varma constructed an agraharam and temple to accommodate brahmins. The idol, originally planned for the temple near ammachi plavu, was installed here owing to the sacred geography of this site.

Sacred geography

By the banks of Neyyar

The Neyyar River — one of the perennial rivers of South Kerala — flows close to the temple, lending the site a natural sanctity that has drawn devotees for generations.

Divine presence

Lord Sree Krishna

ശ്രീ കൃഷ്ണ ഭഗവാൻ
01

The presiding deity, eighth avatar of Lord Vishnu, revered as the Supreme Being in Vaishnavism. Worshipped here in Chaturbhuja form — holding the Sudarshana Chakra, Panchajanya conch, Kaumodaki mace, and a lotus — radiating eternal love, wisdom, and protection.

"Yada yada hi dharmasya, glanir bhavati Bharata
abhyutthanam adharmasya, tadatmanam srijamy aham"

— Bhagavad Gita 4.7

Lord Ganapathy

ഗണപതി ഭഗവാൻ
02

Vighneshwara — the remover of all obstacles. Enshrined within the temple complex, depicted in Gajanana form with a broken tusk, modaka sweet in hand, and his beloved mouse at his feet, symbolising grace over the mightiest obstacles.

"Vakratunda Mahakaya, Suryakoti Samaprabha
Nirvighnam Kuru Me Deva, Sarva Karyeshu Sarvada"

— Ganapathi Sloka
Sacred grove

Nagar Kavu

Adjacent to the temple lies the sacred Nagar Kavu — a serpent grove revered as the abode of Naga Devatas. Nestled near the banks of the Neyyar River, this ancient grove is one of the most spiritually potent spots in the Neyyattinkara region.

The Kavu is shaded by old trees — pala, ilippa, and arali — whose roots intertwine with the earth like the coils of the divine serpent. Stone sculptures of Nagaraja and Nagayakshi stand watch beneath the canopy, garlanded with turmeric and sacred threads by generations of devotees.

Serpent worship in Kerala is an ancient Dravidian tradition. Families come here seeking fertility, family harmony, and relief from Sarpa Dosha. The sound of the flowing Neyyar River amplifies the divine energy of the grove and carries the prayers of devotees forward.

Avani Masa Nagar Pooja

Consecration ceremony of serpent deities performed by temple priests with elaborate rituals, mantras, and offerings of turmeric, milk, and flowers.

Ayilyam Pooja

Special puja performed on Ayilya nakshatra days — the most auspicious time to worship Naga Devatas and seek relief from ancestral curses.

Krishnashtami
Birth anniversary of Lord Krishna, celebrated with great devotion through night-long bhajans, Dahi Handi, and elaborate decorations of the sanctum.

Vinayaka Chaturthi
Joyous birth celebration of Lord Ganapathy. The temple is adorned with flowers and banana leaves, with special modaka offerings, homams, and ashtadravya abhishekam.

Chingam
Vaikunta Ekadasi
Most sacred day in the Vaishnava calendar, marking the opening of the celestial gates. Devotees observe a fast and gather for midnight puja and the symbolic passage through Vaikunta Dwaram.

Mandala Pooja
Sacred 41-day period of intensified worship beginning on the first day of Vrischikam. Each day sees chanting of Sree Vishnu Sahasranam, Suktams — Durga, Sree, Purusha — and Shiva Kavacham.
